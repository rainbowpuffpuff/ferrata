# The light client's unattended run on our public LEZ test network, from public chain data alone

Our public LEZ v0.4.0-rc3 test network (ours, not a network Logos runs; its address is in
`scripts/reproduce/deployed-programs.json`) has carried a Sepolia instance of the attestation program since the
network's current chain began on 23 November 2026, kept current by `ferrata-updater` on the network's host and
watched by `attest-monitor` (EVIDENCE rows 242 and 248). This record reconstructs that run from what anyone can read:
the blocks the network's public RPC serves. Nothing here comes from the host: no logs, no shell, no keys.

The files, all generated by `scripts/verifylib/runrecord.py build` and re-checked by `scripts/verify.py`:

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `transactions.tsv` | One line per transaction to the attestation program (account `6f9zuRR6…`, image `0x816d1fef…`): LEZ block, its time and hash, the transaction's hash, the step (`init`, `open`, `miller`, `adopt`, `finish`, `attest`), applied or reverted (the block's own reverted list), the instance, the job, the signer and its nonce, and for an update the fields decoded from the `Open` that carried it: fork, attested, finalized and signature slots, the finalized header's root, the sync-committee participation, whether it carried the next committee |
| `chain.json` | What the walk saw of the chain: the as-of block (number, hash, time), the number of blocks read, the pauses in block production, each instance's `Init` configuration, and each instance's own counter of applied jobs and finalized slot as the chain served them at the end of the read |
| `summary.json` | Every figure of the run, computed from `transactions.tsv` and `chain.json` |
| `RECORD.md` | The same figures in words and tables: the as-of block, the pauses, each instance's updates, intervals, adoptions, period crossings, refusals and senders |

## Re-check it

```sh
python3 -I scripts/verify.py run-record                 # offline, seconds
python3 -I scripts/verify.py run-record-online          # the network's RPC, read-only; minutes on a first run
python3 -I scripts/verify.py run-record-counters        # the network's RPC, read-only; the instances' own counters
python3 -I scripts/verify.py run-record-beacon          # public Sepolia beacon APIs, read-only; about 3 min
```

- `run-record` (offline) recomputes every figure of `summary.json` from the transaction list and regenerates
  `RECORD.md`, failing on any difference; it also checks the list and `chain.json` against each other (every step
  after its job's `open` or `init` and with its job's update fields; the lines' blocks, times and hashes against the
  as-of block; the pauses the lines' times prove; the instances their applied `Init`s set; the counters their
  applied `finish`es give). It reads no block: what `chain.json` says of the chain itself (the as-of hash, every
  pause) is checked against the chain only by the online checks.
- `run-record-online` takes the attestation program's account from the network's `/programs.json` and its immutable
  loader header on chain (not from the record); reads every block from 1 to the as-of block again, recomputes each
  block's hash from its bytes and checks that each links to the one before; checks the as-of block's hash and time,
  the pauses and each instance's `Init` configuration against `chain.json`; decodes every transaction to the program
  again and compares it with its line, field by field; and fails if a transaction is missing from the list or the
  list holds one the chain does not. Its block cache is `.scratch/verify-cache/lez-blocks/`: delete it to read
  everything again.
- `run-record-counters` reads each instance the chain's applied `Init`s set (they must be the record's instances):
  its own counter of applied jobs and its store's finalized slot, which must equal 1 (`Init`'s activation) plus the
  applied `finish` steps up to the head it reads, and the newest one's finalized slot.
- `run-record-beacon` asks public Sepolia beacon APIs for the block at each applied update's finalized slot and
  checks that its root is the one the update carried, and that it is canonical; a list with no applied update fails.

## What it shows, and what it does not

As of the block `RECORD.md` names, the instance kept current by the host's updater (`3pQmc7V8…`) has had every
update applied by one account, the updater's (`Fdgg7HoW…`), about one per Sepolia epoch; its longest interval is
at a sync-committee period crossing, where an update of the new period waits for the next committee; `RECORD.md`
gives every figure. The second instance (`HAh9gu1B…`) is not an unattended run: it is the instance of the updater
module's test sessions of 23 November (EVIDENCE row 237: `ferrata_updater_module` in a headless Logos Core,
updates sent by its own account `CJtEhDN6…`), two sessions with 4 h 51 min between them, and it was left after the
second; its one refused transaction is an `attest` of that session.

Interruptions the chain shows are listed in `RECORD.md` exactly: the pauses of block production over 60 s (the
sequencer made no block for up to 4 min 13 s, three times), and the intervals between updates. What the chain does
not show, and this record does not claim:

- the host itself: whether the updater's process restarted, its logs, its disk, and why the sequencer paused. The
  host's Logos Blockchain node was disk-bound on 24 and 25 November (the operator's account, not chain data); on
  chain those two days hold 224 updates each (a Sepolia day has 225 epochs), no interval between updates over the
  longest `RECORD.md` names, and one of the three pauses of block production: the one of 25 November at 10:02 UTC
  matches the host's move to a larger instance with a faster data volume, the operator's intervention that ended the
  disk-bound period (the operator's account); the other two pauses have no cause on record;
- when each block became final on the network's Logos Blockchain node: a block's Bedrock status is served as it is
  at the time of the read (every block but the newest few read `Finalized`), not as it was when the block was made;
- anything before the current chain: the network's first chain of 23 November (EVIDENCE row 248) is not served
  any more, so the run counted here starts with this chain's `Init` (block 22, 23 November 10:14 UTC).

Times are the LEZ blocks' own timestamps (milliseconds, UTC), which the sequencer sets; the Sepolia times are the
slots' (genesis time + 12 s a slot). Nothing here is audited.

Ferrata wallet for Android

The wallet of our LEZ v0.4 test network as an Android app. Your keys stay on the phone, unlocked with your fingerprint or face. Send and receive with QR codes, use names, write end-to-end encrypted messages, and get notifications without a push service.

    What the app is

    A Trusted Web Activity: Android opens this site's /wallet/ in Chrome's engine, full screen, without an address bar. The app holds no wallet code of its own, so the app and the web wallet are one program with one review: what you check in the browser is what runs in the app. Android shows it full screen only after checking that this site names the app's signing key in /.well-known/assetlinks.json; otherwise it opens as a normal browser tab with the address shown.

    KeysMade and kept on the phone, in Chrome's storage for this site, encrypted with your passphrase (PBKDF2-SHA-256, 600,000 iterations; AES-256-GCM). Never sent anywhere.
    UnlockPassphrase, or a passkey (fingerprint, face, screen lock: WebAuthn's PRF secret unwraps the key; nothing to guess offline), or a 6-12 digit PIN (erased after 5 wrong tries; weaker against someone who copies the phone's storage).
    NetworkOnly this site: its RPC and faucet. The page's Content-Security-Policy forbids any other server.
    OfflineThe app opens, unlocks and shows your receive QR without a network: its service worker keeps exactly one build, each file checked against its SHA-256.
    NotificationsNo push service: while the app is open, the page asks this network for new blocks (every 15 s) and reads them itself. When it is closed nothing runs.
    NeedsAndroid 7.0 or later with Chrome (or another browser with Trusted Web Activities, such as Edge or Samsung Internet).

    What it checks, and what it does not

    This app runs no node on the phone. An app that carries a light client of the chain checks more than this one, and says so here first:

    Size, battery when closedAbout 1 MB; nothing runs when closedTens of MB; a node may keep syncing

    Messages are end-to-end encrypted with hybrid post-quantum envelopes (X25519 and ML-KEM-768, padded to fixed sizes). They have no forward secrecy: the messaging keys derive from the account's key, so whoever later obtains that key can read past messages. Who wrote to whom, when and the padded size are public on LEZ.

    Build it yourself

    From the repository: HOST=<this host> bash deploy/testnet/web/android/build.sh on Linux with JDK 17 and Node 22. It fetches Google's Android command-line tools (SHA-1 checked against Google's list), generates the project with Bubblewrap's own generator (pinned), builds it twice in two directories and requires the two unsigned APKs to be equal, then signs. The signing key was made on the build machine and is not in the repository; its certificate is above and in assetlinks.json.

Bridge privacy

What stays on your machine, what each chain publishes by design, what an observer can still correlate, and what this host and every helper can see. Sources: docs/trust.md §8, the privacy suite's results (docs/privacy/RESULTS.md) and the field-by-field table docs/privacy/generated/fields.md.

What never leaves your machine

The values that would link a deposit on Ethereum to a balance on LEZ are your seed and what derives from it: the keys kAuth and kView, a deposit's secret s and the opening of its commitment C, a burn's r, sOut and recipient, and your burn counter. On the default, private path none of them leaves your machine and none is in any transaction.

Measured, not asserted. The privacy suite searched every public byte of both chains over 20 round trips for 320 such values and found none, in dev mode and on real proofs of the deployed programs (EVIDENCE rows 23 and 89).

A plaintext claim publishes its whole witness (leaf index, depositor, s, destination). You must choose it explicitly; the tools warn first.

What is public by design

  • The deposit on Ethereum: depositor address, token, denomination, the commitment C. That is the public leg; the bridge hides which LEZ account it pays, not that you deposited.
  • A claim on LEZ: a nullifier, the root it reads, a fee alias; with a relayer, the relayer and its fee.
  • A release on Ethereum: chain, vault, configuration, root, nullifier, token, amount, recipient, relayer, fee.

What this host sees

Every page on this host may run scripts from this host only (its Content-Security-Policy, which scripts/site-manifest.py verify checks), so no page runs another site's script. What else a page reads: the trust pages read only this host, except the verify page's quick check, which also reads the manifest's copy on GitHub; the build page reads api.github.com (CI status); the explorer and status pages read the public beacon and execution endpoints named in the explorer's config.json; the wallet reads this host and your browser wallet. The host's own software (its web server's configuration, the gateway and the faucet) is not in this repository yet; by its configuration:

The gateway's log (with the faucet's lines) and the web server's log file are copied to the operator's CloudWatch log group, kept 30 days by the stack's default (the host's stack definition). A host that wanted to log more could: that is why its code belongs in the repository, and why the wallet's files are listed in the site manifest. To avoid this host entirely, run the tools against your own LEZ node and your own Ethereum endpoint.

Why the web wallet does not prove private transactions. No browser can make a proof of LEZ's privacy circuit today, and sending the witness to someone else's prover hands them your account. Claims, burns and releases are made with the ferrata tool on your own machine. Measured on CPU with LEZ v0.4.0-rc3: a private claim took 264.3 s to prove and a private burn 302.2 s (EVIDENCE row 123); no GPU time for them is recorded.

Ferrata · the test network

Testers: start here

This is a LEZ v0.4.0-rc3 network that we run for the Ferrata bid, bridged to Ethereum’s Sepolia test network; it is not Logos’s LEZ testnet 0.4, which is not released. Nothing here is audited: its tokens are test tokens with no value, and the network may be reset.

Never paste a recovery phrase or a private key anywhere. Read Safety before you start.

What to try, in order

Each step names the page to open and what you should see. If a step does not do what it says, stop there and tell us. The step numbers are the ones the report form uses.

  1. Create a wallet

    Open the wallet and choose “Create a new wallet”. Write the 24 words on paper, tick the box, then pick a passphrase for this browser.

    You should see Home with your account id and a public balance of 0.

  2. Get test coins from the faucet

    On Home, press “Get test coins”. The faucet sends 10 LGO, once per account a day.

    You should see “Received: your balance is 10 LGO.” within a few seconds (6.5 s when we measured it).

  3. Send a transfer: public, then private

    Public. Add a second account (“+ New account”), copy its id from Home, switch back to the first and send it 1 LGO from Send.

    You should see “In block N, applied” within a block or two (a block every 10 s; 8.8 s when we measured it), and both balances change. The fee is a few millionths of an LGO.

    Private (optional, command line). The wallet’s Private tab shows your private account and what proving needs. The transfer itself is proven on your own computer with LEZ’s own wallet, from the same phrase: 49.5 s on a GPU, about 15 minutes on 32 CPU threads.

    You should see it in the explorer as a private transaction: only its public parts, no amount and no accounts.

  4. Names and messages

    A name. On Home, type a name and press “Claim”. The button then counts down for about 4 minutes and becomes “Reveal”: press it within the hour. A name lasts 180 days.

    You should see “NAME.lez is yours” and the name on Home.

    A message. In Inbox, press “Publish my messaging key” on both accounts, then write from one to the other (by account id, receive code or name). Messages are encrypted, but they stay on a public chain: write test text only.

    You should see the message in the other account’s Inbox.

  5. Look it up in the explorer

    Open the explorer and search for your account id, a transaction hash or @name.

    You should see each of your transactions in its block, marked applied. The explorer recomputes every block’s hash in your browser.

  6. The bridge: Sepolia to LEZ and back

    You need MetaMask, or another browser wallet, on Sepolia with a little Sepolia ETH of your own (Limits). How long each stage takes: the table below.

    1. Deposit. In the wallet’s Bridge tab, press “Connect your browser wallet”. Pick ETH, 0.001, and “this wallet’s selected public account”; press “Review the deposit”, then “Deposit with my browser wallet”, and confirm in MetaMask. You should see “Deposited: leaf N in block M”.
    2. Wait for the import, then claim. About 20 minutes later, press “Find my deposits on the vault”, then “Claim on LEZ” (the wallet imports the deposit first if nobody has yet). You should see “Claimed in LEZ block N: 0.001 ETH now held by your account on LEZ”.
    3. Burn. Under “Burn to Ethereum”, pick 0.001 ETH. Leave the recipient empty to use your MetaMask address. Press “Review the burn”, check the address character by character, then “Sign and burn”. You should see “Burned 0.001 ETH in LEZ block N (burn counter i …)”, with the release command.
    4. Release on Sepolia (command line). Not in the web wallet yet, and the hardest step: it needs the ferrata tool built for this network (deploy/testnet/cli-rc3/), your phrase on your own computer, and a Groth16 proof of the release. You can make that proof with RISC Zero’s prover on an x86 computer with Docker; this network’s GPU prover can make it too, but today only its operator can reach it. You should see the vault’s Release event pay your address 0.000997 ETH: 0.001 less its relay fee (0.2 %) and checkpoint fee (0.1 %). This is the least-tested step: tell us where it stops.

  7. Read the latest attested Ethereum facts

    Open the build page’s Live section (it needs JavaScript). The status page shows how far the network’s Sepolia light client is behind. To attest a fact yourself, use the wallet’s Prove tab.

    You should see recent Sepolia facts (an account or a storage slot at a block), each with the LEZ transaction that attested it, linked to the explorer.

How long the bridge takes

Expected times come from the code and the deployment’s configuration; measured ones are from the first deposit and burn on this network, on 23 and 24 November 2026 (UTC).

Sepolia finalizes your deposit, and the network’s light client records it 13–19 min 19 min: deposited 16:12, final 16:29, recorded on LEZ 16:30
The deposit is imported on LEZ when you claim, the wallet imports it itself; the network also imports every 10 min 21 min after the deposit (16:33)
Claim, then burn one LEZ block each, a block every 10 s 8.8 s from sending to the block (a transfer)
Checkpoint: your burn proven to the vault the network looks for burns every 60 s, waits 2 min more for burns sent together, then proves the checkpoint on a GPU and submits it. One checkpoint waits at a time: a burn made during another’s window goes into the next one on Sepolia 25 min after the burn (20:38 to 21:03)

Release delay 2 min after the finalize not measured here yet

Limits

The network is shared, so its gateway limits each address. These are the settings it runs with.

The test network’s limits
WhatLimitWhat you see when you reach it
Faucet 10 LGO a claim; one claim per account a day; 3 claims per IP address a day, failed ones included, and 10 per network (an IPv4 /24 or IPv6 /64); 120 an hour for everyone; one claim at a time per address HTTP 429 and a message, such as “this account was funded recently; again in N s” or “this address has had its claims for today”
Requests to the network (/rpc), per IP address about 10 requests a second, bursts of 60 (a request for many blocks counts more); 6 at once; transactions 0.5 a second, bursts of 12 HTTP 429 with a Retry-After header: the seconds to wait (JSON-RPC error -32005). The wallet and the explorer wait and try again a few times by themselves, then show the error
Transactions, everyone together 0.5 a second, bursts of 10 the same: HTTP 429 with Retry-After
Data the network sends back about 128 KiB a second per IP address, bursts of 4 MiB; one answer at most 8 MiB; at most 50 blocks per request and 20 requests per batch the same: HTTP 429 with Retry-After
Bridge deposits one denomination per deposit: 0.001, 0.01 or 0.1 ETH, plus a small fee the wallet shows before you sign; or the test token TUSD: 100, 1,000 or 10,000 (the Bridge tab mints it for you; you pay the Sepolia gas). The vault takes at most 10 ETH and 1,000,000 TUSD a day in deposits, and the same in releases, for everyone together the vault refuses the deposit: the transaction fails and only its gas is spent
Sepolia ETH bring your own: the faucet here gives LGO on this network only MetaMask reports insufficient funds

Getting Sepolia ETH

The smallest deposit is 0.001 ETH, plus its fee and the gas. Several public Sepolia faucets exist, for example Google Cloud’s Web3 faucet, Alchemy’s, QuickNode’s and pk910’s proof-of-work faucet. We do not run or vouch for any of them, and their terms change.

Browsers, as tested

  • Chromium (through Playwright, desktop and phone-sized screens): every page, and the wallet’s flows end to end, also against this network.
  • Firefox 146: every page loads and was checked for accessibility at desktop width; the wallet’s flows were not run in it.
  • WebKit 26, Safari’s engine (on Linux with an iPhone’s screen, not a real iPhone): creating a wallet, its QR codes, PIN unlock.
  • MetaMask: one bridge deposit, by hand, in a desktop browser; the automated tests use a stand-in browser wallet.
  • Android: the wallet also comes as an app (the Android page).

Other browsers may well work: tell us which one you used.

Safety

  • Never paste a recovery phrase or a private key anywhere: not in an issue, a chat, a form or a terminal. Nobody needs it to help you, and a report never does.
  • Use a fresh wallet made for this test, and a fresh MetaMask account that holds only Sepolia ETH. Never use a phrase or an account that holds anything of value.
  • The wallet shares its web address with this site’s other pages (the build page, the explorer, the status, trust, Basecamp and Android pages, and this one). A script on any of them could read the wallet’s encrypted record and its PIN wrap, ask for your passkey, or drive the wallet once it is unlocked; only the host’s script policy and those pages’ own code stand in the way. The wallet says so on its first screen and under More.
  • The command-line steps (a private transfer, the release) read your test phrase from a file on your own computer. Type it into that file with an editor, never into a command; let only you read the file, and delete it when you are done.
  • Found a security problem? Report it privately, not in a public issue.

How to report

Open a tester report

The report form is on GitHub: it needs a GitHub account, and reports are public. Please include:

  • the step you tried, what happened, and what you expected;
  • the page and its build id: on the build page and on this page, the commit in the footer; in the wallet, More, then App: “Offline copy: build …”; on the Basecamp page, “built from …”;
  • your browser and operating system;
  • transaction hashes, LEZ transaction ids or explorer links, and the time (UTC if you can);
  • screenshots, once you have checked that none shows a recovery phrase, a private key, a passphrase or a PIN.
